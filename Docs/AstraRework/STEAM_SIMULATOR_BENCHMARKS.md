The following are design inferences to test in Geode, rather than measurements of those games:

- A stranger should identify the entrance, business state, checkout and main workbench from ordinary first-person views. Signage reinforces an already readable layout.
- The starter shop should contain a coherent minimum kit and visible room for growth. Buying equipment should alter both capability and the shop's appearance.
- Customer circulation, employee/work routes and machine clearance need explicit space. Decorative density belongs on edges and work surfaces, not in aisles.
- Material and lighting variety should separate clean retail, dirty work, wet work and storage. Mineral surfaces need neutral enough light to retain their identity.
- Management screens should show the decision first: item, image, cost, capacity, prerequisites and physical consequence. Controller focus must follow that hierarchy.
- Cashier work should give a short payoff to processing and sourcing. It should not monopolize the career or force long administrative rituals between rocks.
- Ambient sound should communicate occupancy, machinery and the exterior at appropriate positions. A single constant room-wide noise bed cannot provide that information.
- The 60 FPS and frame-pacing requirements come from the Astra specification. Store screenshots and positive reviews cannot prove performance, audio quality, accessibility, or persistence; those need direct Geode testing and standalone measurements.

Avoid copying any reference's exact layout, proprietary assets, typography, branding, characters, or interface. The original target concepts and the chosen Geode material/architecture system remain a separate production step.
